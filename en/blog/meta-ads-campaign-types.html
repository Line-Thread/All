Which Meta Ads campaign type should you choose?

By Arthur Heusdens · Momenti

The campaign objective you choose in Meta Ads Manager determines how the algorithm optimises, and therefore who sees your ad. Choose the wrong objective, and you're optimising for the wrong action, paying for people who never become customers. It's one of the most underestimated settings on the whole platform.

How the campaign objective works

Meta's algorithm looks for people who take the action you've set as your objective. Choose Sales, and Meta looks for buyers. Choose Traffic, and Meta looks for people who click. Those two groups only partially overlap, and the difference in ad performance is enormous.

Sales - the standard for e-commerce

The Sales objective optimises for purchases, add-to-cart or other conversions on your website. This is the right choice for nearly every webshop looking to generate revenue. Meta actively looks for people who, based on prior behaviour, are most likely to buy.

Required: a correctly installed Meta Pixel or Conversions API, so Meta knows when a conversion has taken place. Without reliable tracking, the Sales objective doesn't work.

Leads, for B2B, service providers and recruitment

The Leads objective is the right choice if you don't have direct sales through the website, but want to generate contact moments: quote requests, sign-ups, job applications.

You have two variants:

  • Instant Forms: the user fills in a form without leaving Meta. Low barrier, high volume, but lead quality is often lower because it's so easy
  • Website conversion: the user clicks through to your landing page and fills in the form there. Higher barrier, lower volume, but the leads are usually better qualified

Traffic, almost never the best choice

With the Traffic objective, Meta optimises for clicks, not buyers or leads. You get cheap clicks, but from people used to clicking without converting. For most goals, this is the wrong objective.

The only situation where Traffic makes sense: when you want to promote an article or page where reading or viewing is the goal, and you can't measure a conversion event.

Awareness, for the long term

Awareness campaigns maximise reach and impressions, not clicks or conversions. Useful if you want to build brand awareness with a new audience and aren't steering directly on conversion. Requires sufficient budget on top of your conversion-focused campaigns - it's not a replacement.

Engagement - a red flag

The Engagement objective optimises for likes, comments and shares. The result: lots of interaction, cheap CPC, and almost no conversions. Engagement metrics say nothing about actual purchase intent or business results.

Yet this objective is still regularly used by agencies wanting to show impressive numbers with minimal effort. Low CPC and high reach look good in a report, but if nothing then happens with those prospects, the ad budget delivered nothing. Watch for this when assessing an agency's reporting.

Advantage+ Shopping Campaigns

Advantage+ Shopping (ASC) is Meta's AI campaign type for e-commerce. It combines prospecting and retargeting automatically and lets Meta make almost all the decisions. It can work well with sufficient budget and strong creative, but deserves its own separate consideration. Read the article on Advantage+ for that.

The rule of thumb

Always optimise for the action closest to revenue. For a webshop, that's a purchase. For a service provider, that's a form submission or phone contact. For a recruiter, that's a job application. Don't settle for an intermediate step just because it's easier to measure - you'll end up paying for people who take that step, not for people who actually become customers.
